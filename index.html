Our Farm & Heritage

Three generations.
One Willamette Valley farm.

Founded in 1985 by Bill Darm, Yamhill County Mushrooms has grown from a modest family operation into one of the Pacific Northwest's premier mushroom growers. His son Bob Darm took the helm and directs daily farm operations today alongside long-time foreman Leonides Perez and Bob's grown sons.

Real mushroom farming starts with the substrate. We formulate and cure our own compost on-site using Oregon agricultural straw and nutrients. In our enclosed composting facility, natural biological activity generates intense heat, pasteurizing the medium before it fills our stacked wooden cedar grow beds.

From there, the beds enter climate-controlled darkrooms where temperature, relative humidity, and air circulation are monitored around the clock. Every mushroom is harvested by hand, stem-trimmed at the bed edge, and packed into our iconic purple cases before same-day transfer to our 34°F cold storage facility.

Split Operation Transparency & Certification Discipline

Our brown varieties (Crimini and Portobello, whole and precision-sliced) are certified 100% Organic by Oregon Tilth (OT-011161) under USDA NOP and US/Canada Equivalence standards. Our white buttons are grown conventionally in the same barns using identical pasteurization and strict commercial foodservice sanitation.

Botanical & Regulatory Note: Agaricus bisporus is a single species harvested across three distinct growth phases: young white button, intermediate brown crimini, and fully matured portabella. Our brown varieties (crimini + portabella, whole and sliced) are certified organic under Oregon Tilth (Client ID OT-011161, USDA NOP). Wild Mushroom Disclosure: True Oregon chanterelles (*Cantharellus*) and morels (*Morchella*) are mycorrhizal wild fungi that cannot be commercially cultivated indoors; when brokered seasonally, wild mushrooms are sourced exclusively through certified commercial foragers operating under Oregon Health Authority (OHA) 90-day documentation guidelines.

Executive Chef Receiving & Walk-In Storage Standards

To protect cellular crispness, cap density, and the full 14-day commercial shelf life of Yamhill County Mushrooms:

Optimal Temperature 34°F – 38°F (1°C – 3°C)

Maintain steady cold-chain in produce walk-in. Never freeze fresh raw mushrooms.

Breathable Storage Purple Cases or Paper Bags

Leave in ventilated corrugated master cases. Never store in airtight plastic bins.

Dry Prep Invariant Brush or Quick Wipe Only

Mushrooms are 90%+ water sponges. Wipe with damp towel right before searing; never soak.

High-Heat Maillard Avoid Pan Crowding

Give sliced caps space to brown. Crowded pans steam rather than develop deep savory umami crust.
