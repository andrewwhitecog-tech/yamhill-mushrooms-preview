Our Story

Three generations.
One farm.

Bill Darm started it in 1985. His son Bob bought it a few years later and runs it today with Leo Perez and his grown kids.

We make our own compost on site. It's loud, it's warm, and it smells like a real farm. Then the compost goes into darkened barns where the mushrooms do their thing for a couple of weeks.

Two varieties we're known for: crimini and portabella. Plus whole and sliced whites when the beds call for them.

— Bob Darm, owner
Our Mushrooms

One species. Three stages. A few specialties.

White, crimini, and portabella are all Agaricus bisporus — the same mushroom at different points in its life. We also grow shiitake, oyster, and chanterelle.

Specialty

Shiitake · oyster · chanterelle

Small runs in 5lb cases for chefs who want something different.

A note on naming: Agaricus bisporus is one species at different maturity stages — harvested young it's a white button, harvested at its brown-capped stage it's crimini, and left to fully open it becomes portabella. Our brown varieties (crimini + portabella) are certified organic by Oregon Tilth. Whites are grown conventionally to food-service standards in the same barns, same compost.
